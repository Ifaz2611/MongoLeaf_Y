    02 — The field guide

    Eight conditions,
    one scan away.

    The model was trained to recognise the conditions below. Learn the visual signs so you can double-check a diagnosis in the orchard.

    • Healthy

      Healthy leaf

      Uniform green, glossy surface with no lesions, spots, curling or powdery growth.

      Keep monitoring new growth
    • Fungal

      Anthracnose

      Dark, sunken spots on leaves, twigs and fruit; lesions can merge into larger patches.

      Thrives in warm, wet weather
    • Bacterial

      Bacterial Canker

      Raised, corky lesions often ringed by a yellow halo; shoots may wilt and die back.

      Spreads with rain splash
    • Pest

      Cutting Weevil

      Notched, ragged leaf edges where adult weevils have fed on the leaf margin.

      Look for feeding at night
    • Fungal

      Die Back

      Shoots brown from the tip downward; dry, brittle twigs and sparse foliage.

      Often follows stress or injury
    • Pest

      Gall Midge

      Small, blister-like galls on leaves and new shoots; distorted, stunted growth.

      Check tender new flush
    • Fungal

      Powdery Mildew

      White, powdery coating on the leaf surface, often starting in scattered patches.

      Improve airflow through the canopy
    • Fungal

      Sooty Mould

      Black, sooty film on leaves, usually growing on honeydew left by sap-sucking insects.

      Treat the insects, not just the mould

    03 — Process

    From photo to plan
    in three steps.

    1. 01

      Upload a clear photo

      Drag in a JPG, PNG or GIF up to 10 MB, or paste a screenshot straight from your clipboard.

    2. 02

      Let the model look

      The image is resized, normalized and passed through a trained convolutional network.

    3. 03

      Read the result

      Get the most likely condition, how confident the model is, and practical next steps.

    A note on the results

    Useful guidance, not a lab report.

    A photo only captures so much. Lighting, angle and leaf age can all sway a prediction. Treat the diagnosis as a strong first opinion — and confirm anything unusual with your local agricultural extension officer before applying chemicals.
